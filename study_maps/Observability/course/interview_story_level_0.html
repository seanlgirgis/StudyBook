From Enterprise Capacity Monitoring to Cloud Observability

1. 30-second version

My background is in enterprise monitoring, APM-style workflows, capacity monitoring, telemetry, dashboards, thresholds, forecasting, RCA, and business reporting. The cloud observability world uses newer tools and patterns, but many of the core ideas are familiar: collect signals, understand system behavior, detect risk, and communicate clearly. I am modernizing that foundation into OpenTelemetry, Prometheus, Grafana, CloudWatch, Dynatrace, Splunk, Datadog, and Kubernetes monitoring.

2. 2-minute version

In my earlier enterprise work, I dealt with monitoring and capacity from an operational and business-impact angle. The work involved telemetry, thresholds, dashboards, capacity headroom, forecasted risk, RCA support, and reporting that helped teams make decisions. Some of the older tools and environments included APM and monitoring platforms such as Dynatrace AppMon, Gomez, CA APM/Wily, AppDynamics, and BMC TrueSight/TSCO.

Modern cloud observability changes the tooling, but not the purpose. Instead of only looking at isolated server or application metrics, teams now connect metrics, logs, traces, dashboards, alerts, and runbooks across distributed systems. OpenTelemetry helps standardize telemetry collection. Prometheus and Grafana are common in metrics and dashboards. CloudWatch is important for AWS. Platforms like Dynatrace, Splunk, and Datadog bring many signals together into APM and observability workflows.

So my bridge is practical. I already understand why telemetry matters, how capacity risk is communicated, and how dashboards support operational and management decisions. I am now updating that experience into cloud-native observability tools and patterns.

3. Risk-safe version

I would not present myself as someone who has owned every modern observability platform in production. My strongest base is enterprise capacity engineering and APM-style monitoring. I am building the modern tooling layer deliberately, with OpenTelemetry, Prometheus, Grafana, CloudWatch, Dynatrace, Splunk, Datadog, and Kubernetes monitoring as the next layer on top of that foundation.
