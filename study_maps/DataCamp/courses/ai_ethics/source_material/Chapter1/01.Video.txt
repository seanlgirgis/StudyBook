4. Why AI ethics?
01:28 - 02:26
Why do we need AI ethics? Imagine an AI model that processes insurance claims and begins disproportionately denying claims from a particular neighborhood. The data used to create the AI is created by humans, and unknowingly, their interests and biases can seep into the decision-making process. Without ethical guidelines, these biases can go unchecked, resulting in unintended harm. It could lead to an entire neighborhood's insurance claims being denied. Moreover, biased or unethical use of AI can impact a wide range of areas. Some may argue that AI is meant to create a fairer criminal justice system, but AI itself is often not genuinely neutral. Therefore, clear guidelines on AI transparency, fairness, and risk are necessary to safeguard these processes.

5. Ethics in practice
02:26 - 03:05
So, how do we bring ethics into AI? It's about aligning AI systems with ethical principles. For instance, a principle of fairness would mean our hypothetical insurance model treats all claims equally, irrespective of the neighborhood. Insurance, finance, and banking firms have established guidelines on training AI algorithms using diverse databases and protecting user data. By setting ethical boundaries and safeguards, customers can receive fair and unbiased results. This highlights the potential of AI when built correctly.

6. The big picture
03:05 - 03:43
Understanding AI ethics enables us to navigate the complex landscape of AI. It goes beyond avoiding harm or bias, although these aspects are crucial. It is also about accountability— who is responsible if an AI system malfunctions? How can we ensure accountability not only for AI's outcomes but also for its construction? Moreover, transparency plays a key role— can we understand the rationale behind an AI's decision? This requires transparency and literacy in both the development and the results produced by AI.
