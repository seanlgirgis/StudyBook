Self-Test Questions

1. A hiring model omits age but uses graduation year. What is the concern?

Graduation year may act as a proxy for age. Removing the protected field does not guarantee fairness.

2. A model is 94% accurate overall but 62% accurate for one group. What should happen?

Investigate the subgroup disparity before trusting or deploying the model.

3. Who remains responsible when AI causes harm?

The people and organizations that build, approve, deploy, use, and monitor it remain accountable.

4. What does transparency cover?

The full lifecycle: data, preparation, model behavior, evaluation, deployment, limitations, decisions, and lessons learned.

5. When is LIME a good choice?

When explaining one specific prediction through a simple local approximation.

6. When is SHAP a good choice?

When showing and comparing how much each feature contributed to predictions.

7. What is automation bias?

Overtrusting an automated result and failing to apply human judgment or verification.

8. Why involve stakeholders beyond developers?

Different groups reveal legal, operational, domain, accessibility, privacy, and lived-impact risks that developers may miss.

9. What is Ethical AI by Design?

Embedding ethics into objectives, stakeholders, data, design, testing, complaint handling, monitoring, and iteration from the beginning.

10. Why are complaint and appeal channels essential?

They let affected people challenge errors, provide missing context, request human review, and reveal real-world harms.

11. Is equal treatment always fair?

No. Identical treatment can still disadvantage people when access, context, or starting conditions differ.

12. How can ethical AI create business value?

It reduces legal, reputational, and operational risk while strengthening trust, loyalty, adoption, and decision quality.

13. What is the black-box problem?

The system produces outputs, but its reasoning is difficult to understand, validate, or challenge.

14. What should happen after deployment?

Continue monitoring performance, fairness, drift, complaints, safeguards, and changing conditions; then correct and iterate.

15. Give the full fairness sequence.

Plan representation early, inspect the collected data, test group outcomes, investigate proxies, and monitor after launch.

Scoring

  • 13–15: Strong course recall.
  • 10–12: Developing; review missed distinctions.
  • 0–9: Repeat Certification Prep and chapter memory reviews.
Mastery rule: A platform pass is not permanent mastery. Retake this test after several days without notes.
