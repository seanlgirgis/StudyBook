AI Ethics Certification Prep

A concentrated review of the distinctions, scenario patterns, and interview-safe explanations most likely to be tested after this course.

1. Must-Know Definitions

Fairness

Look for unjustified differences in outcomes or errors. Do not confuse equal process with equitable impact.

Accountability

Assign responsibility to humans and organizations for decisions, monitoring, correction, and appeal.

Transparency

Make the AI lifecycle and consequential decisions understandable to relevant stakeholders.

Explainable AI

Help humans understand why a model produced a prediction, using interpretable models or post-hoc methods.

2. High-Value Distinctions

Fairness vs. identical treatment

Same treatment can be unfair when access, context, or outcomes differ.

Protected field vs. proxy

Removing age does not help if graduation year reveals age. Test outcomes, not only columns.

Production vs. Use Team

Production builds and tests. Use applies, verifies, supervises, reports, and overrides.

LIME vs. SHAP

LIME approximates one local case. SHAP assigns contribution values and supports comparisons.

3. Scenario Decision Rules

  1. Higher impact → stronger explanation and human review.
  2. More personal data → stronger privacy justification, minimization, consent, and control.
  3. Strong overall accuracy + weak subgroup performance → investigate before trusting.
  4. No complaint or appeal path → governance is incomplete.
  5. Ethics checked only after launch → not Ethical AI by Design.

4. Ethical AI by Design — Seven Checkpoints

  1. Responsible objectives
  2. Stakeholder participation
  3. Responsible data collection and management
  4. Transparent design and human control
  5. Bias and group-performance evaluation
  6. Feedback, complaint, review, and appeal channels
  7. Monitoring, auditing, and iteration

5. Certification Traps

Watch for absolute claims. “Removing race guarantees fairness,” “AI is objective,” “one audit is enough,” and “the algorithm is responsible” are all warning signs.
  • Choose answers that preserve human responsibility.
  • Prefer lifecycle transparency over a vague accuracy claim.
  • Prefer representative data plus outcome testing over column removal alone.
  • Prefer stakeholder involvement throughout the lifecycle.
  • Prefer monitoring and correction after deployment.

6. Interview-Safe Summary

“I evaluate responsible AI across the full lifecycle. I start with ethical objectives and stakeholder participation, examine data representation and proxies, test group-level outcomes, choose explainability appropriate to the impact, assign accountable owners, preserve human review and appeal, and monitor the system after deployment.”
