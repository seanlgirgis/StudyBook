2. Course Big Picture

AI ethics asks whether an AI system should be built and used—not merely whether it can make accurate predictions. Responsible AI combines technical quality with human values, clear ownership, understandable decisions, fair outcomes, and ongoing governance.

Values and objectives ↓ Stakeholders → Data → Model → Decision → Real-world impact ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ participation fairness explanation accountability \________ monitoring and iteration ________/
Course punchline: Responsible AI is a lifecycle practice: define ethical goals, use representative data, test outcomes, explain decisions, preserve human responsibility, listen to affected people, and keep monitoring.

3. Core Concepts

Fairness

Check whether outcomes or errors unjustifiably disadvantage people or groups. Equal treatment alone does not guarantee fair impact.

Accountability

People and organizations remain responsible for design, approval, deployment, monitoring, correction, and appeal.

Transparency

Explain data, preparation, model behavior, limitations, deployment, and important decisions to relevant stakeholders.

Explainability

Use interpretable models or methods such as LIME and SHAP to clarify why a model produced a result.

Stakeholder Participation

Include technical, legal, operational, domain, user, and affected-community perspectives throughout the lifecycle.

Ethical AI by Design

Build ethics into objectives, data, design, testing, complaint handling, monitoring, and iteration from the start.

4. Core Syntax and Patterns

High-impact AI review

1. Who is affected?
2. What harm is possible?
3. Is the data representative?
4. Can the decision be explained?
5. Who owns the outcome?
6. Can a human review or override?
7. How will it be monitored?

Use this sequence before approving AI in healthcare, hiring, lending, insurance, legal, or other consequential settings.

Fairness validation

Plan representation → inspect data → test group outcomes → investigate proxies → monitor drift

Removing a protected attribute is not enough; proxies and unequal error rates may remain.

Explainability choice

Interpretable model = understandable by design
LIME = local approximation for one prediction
SHAP = feature contribution values

Choose the explanation approach according to decision impact, audience, model type, and need for local or comparative explanations.

5. Comparisons and Decision Rules

Fairness through unawareness

Remove sensitive attributes. Helpful as one control, but weak when proxies remain or outcomes are not audited.

Outcome-based fairness review

Compare decisions and errors across groups, investigate disparities, and repeat checks over time.

Situation Prefer Reason
High-impact decision Stronger explanation and human review The consequences of error are serious.
Need one local explanation LIME It approximates behavior around one prediction.
Need comparable feature contributions SHAP It assigns contribution values to features.

6. Common Mistakes

Assuming AI is neutral Data, objectives, labels, thresholds, and deployment choices all carry human values and possible bias.
Equating identical treatment with fairness Fairness examines unjust disadvantage and real outcomes, not only whether the same process was applied.
Removing sensitive columns and stopping Search for proxies and compare group-level outcomes and error rates.
Treating ethics as a launch gate Ethics continues through complaints, monitoring, audits, correction, and iteration.

7. Interview Translation

What is AI ethics? AI ethics is the practice of aligning AI development and use with human values such as fairness, privacy, transparency, accountability, safety, and respect for affected people.
Why is removing protected attributes insufficient? Other variables may act as proxies, and unfairness can still appear in group-level outcomes. Fairness must be tested, not assumed.
How do LIME and SHAP differ? LIME creates a simple local approximation around one prediction. SHAP assigns contribution values showing how features pushed a prediction.
What is Ethical AI by Design? It means embedding ethical objectives, stakeholder participation, responsible data practices, transparency, bias testing, complaint channels, monitoring, and iteration throughout the lifecycle.
