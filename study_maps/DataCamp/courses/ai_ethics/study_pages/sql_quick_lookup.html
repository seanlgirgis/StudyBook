Core Principles

Fairness

Check whether decisions or errors unjustifiably disadvantage people or groups.

Trap: identical treatment does not always produce fair outcomes.

Accountability

Assign human and organizational responsibility for design, use, monitoring, correction, and appeal.

Trap: “the algorithm decided” does not remove responsibility.

Transparency

Explain data, preparation, model behavior, limitations, deployment, and important decisions.

Trap: saying only “the model is accurate” is not meaningful transparency.

Privacy

Collect only needed data, explain its purpose, protect it, and give users meaningful control.

Trap: more data may improve personalization while increasing harm.

Human Oversight

Preserve review, override, escalation, and appeal for consequential AI decisions.

Trap: automation bias can cause people to trust incorrect outputs.

Continuous Monitoring

Recheck performance, fairness, complaints, drift, and safeguards after deployment.

Trap: pre-launch approval is not permanent ethical validation.

Bias and Fairness

Historical Bias

Past decisions in training data may encode old discrimination or unequal opportunity.

Ask whether the past should be copied, not only predicted.

Fairness Through Unawareness

Remove sensitive attributes such as race, gender, or age.

Useful but insufficient because proxies may remain.

Proxy Variable

A feature that indirectly reveals a sensitive attribute, such as ZIP code for race or graduation year for age.

Test outcomes; do not infer fairness from column names.

Explainable AI

Black Box

Inputs and outputs are visible, but the internal reasoning is difficult to understand.

Higher-impact decisions require stronger explanations.

Interpretable Model

A model understandable by design, such as a decision tree or linear model.

May be preferred when justification matters more than marginal accuracy gains.

LIME

Builds a simple local approximation around one prediction.

Memory: LIME = local explanation.

SHAP

Assigns feature-contribution values showing how inputs pushed a prediction.

Memory: SHAP = contribution values.

Governance and Design

AI Production Team

Builds, trains, tests, documents, and improves the AI system.

Production builds it.

AI Use Team

Applies the system in real work, verifies results, reports problems, and overrides when needed.

Use operates and supervises it.

Ethical Framework

Values and decision criteria used to evaluate AI proposals consistently.

There is no single framework for every organization.

Stakeholder Participation

Bring technical, legal, operational, domain, customer, user, and affected-community views into the lifecycle.

No single team sees every ethical risk.

Ethical AI by Design

Embed ethics in objectives, stakeholders, data, design, testing, feedback, monitoring, and iteration.

Do not bolt ethics on after deployment.
