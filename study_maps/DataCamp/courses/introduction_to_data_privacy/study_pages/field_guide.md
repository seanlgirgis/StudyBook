## Whole-course model

Privacy governs whether personal data is collected, used, shared, retained, and deleted appropriately. Security protects the data. Privacy by Design embeds those requirements early. Classification, risk analysis, de-identification, lifecycle controls, laws, and accountability turn the principles into an operating program.

## Core memory rules

- Security protects; privacy governs.
- Strong security does not automatically mean strong privacy.
- CIA = confidentiality, integrity, availability.
- PREACH = Purpose, Right to Request, Easy to understand, Alerting, Consent, How.
- Privacy by Design = proactive, private by default, embedded, full functionality, end-to-end security, transparency, and respect for users.
- Classification without matching handling controls is decoration.
- Pseudonymized data can still be personal data.
- Removing names does not guarantee anonymity.
- Protect data from collection through verified deletion.
- jAsper = Jurisdiction, Author/Aim, Scope, Penalty, Enforcement, Requirements.
- Jurisdiction is where a law reaches; scope is who and what it covers.
- Enforcement is who acts; penalty is the consequence.
- Shift left means asking privacy questions before the design becomes difficult to change.
- Privacy promises need operational evidence.

## Practical privacy review

```text
Purpose
-> Minimize
-> Classify
-> Assess risk
-> Limit access
-> De-identify where useful
-> Protect through the lifecycle
-> Retain only as needed
-> Delete securely
-> Monitor and prove accountability
```

## Interview-safe summary

Privacy is broader than security. Security protects information from unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure, and disruption. Privacy also asks whether personal information should be collected, whether its use is transparent and proportionate, what rights the person has, how long the data is retained, and whether the organization can prove its controls operate effectively.
