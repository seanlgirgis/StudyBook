2. Course Big Picture

Privacy governs whether personal data is collected, used, shared, retained, and deleted appropriately. Security protects that data. Privacy by Design embeds those expectations into products and processes early, while classification, risk analysis, de-identification, lifecycle controls, laws, and accountability make the program operational.

PERSONAL DATA -> purpose and minimization -> classification and risk -> access, security, and de-identification -> use and sharing -> retention and deletion -> rights, laws, evidence, and accountability Build privacy in early; prove it with operational evidence.
Course punchline: Strong security is necessary but not sufficient: privacy also requires justified purpose, transparency, user control, limited use, proportionate retention, and accountability.

3. Core Concepts

Privacy vs. Security

Security protects data; privacy governs whether its handling is appropriate. A control may support both when it protects personal data.

PREACH

Purpose, Right to Request, Easy to understand, Alerting, Consent, and How the data is used or shared.

CIA Triad

Confidentiality controls access, integrity preserves trustworthiness, and availability keeps systems and data reachable.

Privacy by Design

Be proactive, private by default, embedded, fully functional, secure end to end, transparent, and respectful of users.

Classification and Risk

Classify sensitivity, then assess likelihood and impact. Labels matter only when handling controls follow them.

Law and Accountability

Use jAsper to understand laws and retain evidence that the privacy program actually operates.

4. Core Syntax and Patterns

Privacy review pattern

Purpose -> Minimize -> Classify -> Assess risk -> Limit access -> De-identify -> Protect -> Retain -> Delete

Use this sequence before launch and throughout the data lifecycle.

jAsper law analysis

J Jurisdiction
A Author and Aim
S Scope
P Penalty
E Enforcement
R Requirements

This separates where a law reaches, what it covers, who enforces it, and what organizations must do.

Lifecycle control map

Collect: notice + minimization
Store: encryption + access
Use: purpose limitation
Share: authorization + secure transfer
Archive: retention + restricted access
Delete: verified disposal

Different lifecycle stages require different controls.

5. Comparisons and Decision Rules

Pseudonymization

Identifiers are replaced with codes, but a separate key can reconnect the records. The data remains personal data.

Anonymization

The goal is to make reasonable re-identification no longer possible. Removing names alone is not enough.

Question Use this idea Main trap
Who can see or change the data? Security / CIA / least privilege Authorized access can still be misused for an unauthorized purpose.
Should we collect, use, or retain it? Privacy / minimization / purpose limitation Secure storage does not justify unnecessary collection or indefinite retention.
Which law applies and what must we do? jAsper Do not confuse scope with jurisdiction or enforcement with penalty.

6. Common Mistakes

Security equals privacy Securely misusing data is still a privacy failure.
Removing names makes data anonymous Indirect identifiers and unique combinations can still enable re-identification.
A policy proves compliance Policies need operational evidence: inventories, reviews, logs, training, vendor assessments, and audits.
New technology removes old obligations Cloud and blockchain introduce new risks; they do not remove privacy rights, retention duties, or accountability.

7. Interview Translation

How do privacy and security differ? Security protects data and systems from unauthorized actions. Privacy governs whether personal data is collected and processed for a valid purpose with transparency, authorization, limited use, and user control.
What is Privacy by Design? It means embedding privacy into requirements, defaults, architecture, interfaces, lifecycle controls, and monitoring from the beginning rather than adding it near release.
How do you assess data risk? Classify the data, evaluate likelihood and impact, then reduce risk through minimization, access control, encryption, de-identification, monitoring, retention limits, and secure deletion.
What does shift left mean for privacy? Ask privacy questions before the design becomes difficult to change, especially for technologies such as blockchain where deletion or correction may be hard.
