1. Big Picture

Privacy gives people meaningful control over how their personal data is collected, used, changed, shared, and protected. Security supplies the protective foundation. A mature program needs both: security prevents unauthorized activity, while privacy also asks whether authorized activity is appropriate, transparent, necessary, and aligned with the individual's choices.

PERSONAL DATA | +--> SECURITY: Can unauthorized people access, alter, or disrupt it? | +--> Confidentiality | +--> Integrity | +--> Availability | +--> PRIVACY: Is the processing valid, necessary, transparent, and authorized? +--> Purpose +--> Rights +--> Consent +--> Notice and understandable policies Strong data protection = Security controls + Privacy controls

Privacy, Personal Data, and PREACH

Privacy concerns a person's rights and control; data privacy applies that idea to the collection and use of personal information.

Information Security and the CIA Triad

Security protects information through confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

Security and Privacy Together

The two disciplines overlap but answer different questions about personal data.

Responsibilities and Incidents

Individuals, companies, and regulators each have responsibilities, and one incident may be both a security and privacy failure.

Section punchline: Security protects the data; privacy governs whether collecting and using that data is justified, understandable, and authorized.

2. Privacy, Personal Data, and PREACH

Modern privacy discussions grew from concerns that technology and publishing could intrude on personal rights. Data privacy narrows the focus to personal information and the individual's ability to influence its collection and use. Personal data includes information related to a person or capable of identifying one, such as a name, date of birth, or geolocation.

PREACH maturity check

  • P — Purpose: Why is the data needed?
  • R — Right to Request: Can the person access, correct, remove, or otherwise change the data?
  • E — Easy to understand: Are the policy and practices clear?
  • A — Alerting: Will people be notified when their data is mishandled?
  • C — Consent: Did the person give meaningful permission?
  • H — How: How will the organization use or share the data?

PREACH privacy review
Purpose: Is this collection necessary for the service?
Right to Request: Can the user correct or delete the data?
Easy to understand: Is the notice written clearly?
Alerting: Will the user be told about misuse or a breach?
Consent: Did the user actively agree?
How: Is use limited to what was explained?
What to notice: A company can secure data perfectly and still violate privacy by collecting too much, using it for an undisclosed purpose, or denying users meaningful choices.

3. Information Security and the CIA Triad

Information security protects information and systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction. The CIA triad organizes this work into three goals:

  • Confidentiality: Only authorized people and systems can access information. Identity and access management is a common control.
  • Integrity: Information remains trustworthy and is not altered unexpectedly. Hashing helps detect changes; encryption protects data from being read by unauthorized parties.
  • Availability: Systems and information remain accessible when needed. Business continuity, disaster recovery, backups, and resilient infrastructure support this goal.
Confidentiality -> IAM, least privilege, encryption
Integrity       -> hashing, validation, change controls
Availability    -> backups, redundancy, BCDR planning
Security-model clarification: The CIA triad is a foundational security model that helps organizations build scalable security programs, but it is not the only model and should not be treated as universally “the best.” The course also introduces DIE as an example of a newer security model.
Common trap: Encryption and hashing are related but not interchangeable. Encryption is designed to be reversible with a key; cryptographic hashing is normally one-way and is often used to detect change.

4. Security and Privacy Together

Security and privacy both protect user data and may use some of the same controls, including encryption and access management. Their emphasis differs. Security asks whether information is protected from unauthorized actions. Privacy asks whether personal information is being processed for a valid purpose, with suitable authorization, transparency, and user control.

Dependency rule: Privacy depends on security because people cannot exercise meaningful control over data that an organization cannot protect.

Exercise classification: Privacy, Security, or Both

Statement or theme Category Why
Right to know and right to be forgotten Privacy These are individual rights concerning personal data.
Empowering individuals to control their own data Privacy The focus is personal choice, rights, and control.
Protecting personal data Both Privacy defines appropriate handling, while security supplies protective controls.
Controls to prevent personal-data exfiltration Both Exfiltration prevention is a security control that also protects privacy when personal data is involved.
Protecting data and systems through confidentiality, integrity, and availability Security This is the core focus of the CIA triad.
Exercise trap: A control can originate in security and still belong under Both when it directly protects personal data and therefore supports privacy outcomes.
Area Primary question Examples
Security Can unauthorized parties access, alter, disclose, or disrupt the information? IAM, encryption, hashing, backups, BCDR

5. Worked Example

Tiffz Backpackz collects survey responses and photographs of customers wearing backpacks. It also stores product information and patents. Classify the controls the company needs.

Data: customer survey responses
Security controls:
  - Restrict access with IAM
  - Encrypt stored and transmitted data
  - Monitor for unauthorized access
Privacy controls:
  - Explain why responses are collected
  - Obtain consent
  - Allow correction or deletion when applicable
  - Do not reuse the responses for an undisclosed purpose

Data: backpack patent
Security controls:
  - Restrict access and protect integrity
Privacy controls:
  - Usually not applicable unless the record also contains personal data
Result: The same dataset may need both security and privacy controls. Non-personal business information may need strong security without creating the same privacy obligations as identifiable customer data.

6. Validation and Troubleshooting

  • Identify whether the information is personal data before deciding which privacy controls apply.
  • For every data use, state the purpose, the authorization or consent basis, and how the use was communicated.
  • Map each security control to confidentiality, integrity, or availability instead of treating the CIA triad as vocabulary only.
  • When reviewing an incident, ask two questions: Was there unauthorized access or alteration? Did the event violate the individual's expected or authorized use of the data?
Red flag: Treating compliance language or a privacy notice as proof that data is protected. Policies do not replace effective security controls, and security controls do not replace valid privacy practices.

7. Common Mistakes

Privacy and security are the same thing They overlap, but security focuses on protection from unauthorized activity while privacy also governs appropriate, authorized, and transparent processing of personal data.
All information is personal data Product specifications and patents may be sensitive business information without being personal data. The classification depends on whether the information relates to or can identify a person.
A security breach is only a technical problem When personal data is stolen, disclosed, or altered without permission, the event is usually both a security failure and a privacy failure.
The CIA triad is the only or universally best security model The CIA triad is foundational and widely useful, but organizations may use other models as well. The course specifically identifies DIE as an example of a newer security model.
Data-exfiltration prevention belongs only to security Preventing exfiltration is a security control, but when the protected information is personal data, the control also supports privacy. In the exercise, this belongs under Both.

8. Interview Translation

How would you explain the difference between privacy and security? Security protects information from unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure, and disruption. Privacy governs whether personal information is collected and processed for a valid purpose, with appropriate transparency, authorization, and user control. Strong privacy therefore depends on strong security, but security alone is not enough.
What is the CIA triad? It is a security model built around confidentiality, integrity, and availability. Confidentiality limits access, integrity keeps information trustworthy and detects unauthorized change, and availability keeps systems and data accessible when needed.
How would you quickly assess a company's privacy program? I would ask PREACH questions: the purpose of collection, user rights to request changes, whether explanations are easy to understand, how users are alerted, whether consent is meaningful, and how the data is actually used or shared.
Is the CIA triad the only security model? No. It is a foundational model for confidentiality, integrity, and availability, but it is not the only model or automatically the best choice in every situation. Other models, including newer approaches such as DIE, may complement it.

9. Memory Review

Section summary
  1. Personal data relates to a person or can help identify one.
  2. PREACH = Purpose, Right to Request, Easy to understand, Alerting, Consent, How.
  3. CIA = Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability.
  4. Security prevents unauthorized actions; privacy also judges authorized actions and user control.
  5. The CIA triad is foundational, not exclusive; DIE is introduced as a newer model.
  6. Security controls such as exfiltration prevention may support both security and privacy when personal data is involved.

Quick self-check

  • Can you explain why a fully secured dataset could still be used in a privacy-violating way?
  • Can you classify IAM, hashing, encryption, backups, and disaster recovery under the CIA triad?
  • Can you apply PREACH to a shopping application that collects purchase history and geolocation?
  • Can you explain why theft of personal data is both a security and a privacy incident?
  • Can you explain why the CIA triad is important without calling it the only security model?
  • Can you correctly place personal-data protection and exfiltration prevention under Both?
Lab decision: No large runnable lab is required. Use scenario-based practice: classify information, apply PREACH, map controls to the CIA triad, and analyze whether an incident is a security issue, a privacy issue, or both.
Completion standard: You can define privacy and personal data, apply PREACH, explain each CIA component with controls, recognize that CIA is foundational rather than exclusive, and distinguish Privacy, Security, and Both in realistic scenarios.
