# What Good Answers Should Include

## Expected Answer Traits
- Accurate to the synthetic policy documents.
- Clear and concise language with next action.
- No invented pricing, guarantees, or unsupported promises.
- Correct service-scope boundary handling.

## Citation Expectations
- Cite 1-3 relevant source docs for normal questions.
- Cite escalation or fallback policy when refusing or routing.
- Avoid citation overload; only include documents that directly support the answer.

## Fallback Expectations
- Use fallback when request is outside scope, outside area, or policy-disallowed.
- Explain why in plain language.
- Offer next best step (waitlist callback, supported service option, or dispatch review).

## Escalation Expectations
- Escalate safety, pricing exceptions, and dispatch-review scenarios.
- Identify escalation reason explicitly.
- Preserve key intake details for handoff.

## Good vs Bad Examples

Good:
- "Plano is in our core coverage zone, so we can proceed with scheduling. I need your zip code, issue summary, and preferred time window. Sources: `service_area.md`, `scheduling_policy.md`."

Bad:
- "Yes we can definitely come any time and fix everything for a discount."
Reason bad: overpromises availability and pricing not supported by policy.

Good:
- "For a 17-year-old A/C system, we usually compare repair vs replacement based on repair cost ratio and failure history. We can schedule an estimate review rather than quote exact chat pricing. Sources: `ac_replacement_estimates.md`, `hvac_repair_policy.md`."

Bad:
- "You must replace it now for $12,000."
Reason bad: invented exact pricing and no policy-grounded explanation.
