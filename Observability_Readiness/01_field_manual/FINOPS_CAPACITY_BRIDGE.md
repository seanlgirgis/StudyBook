# FinOps Capacity Bridge

## 1. Why FinOps belongs in the BOA capacity story
Capacity forecasting and FinOps meet at planning decisions. Forecasting shows
usage trend, headroom, ownership, and risk timing. FinOps-style discussions use
that evidence to align engineering, finance, and business priorities around
cost, reliability, and service value.

## 2. What FinOps teams care about
- Cost visibility by service, application, team, or platform lane.
- Usage trend and projected demand windows.
- Financial impact of under-capacity versus over-provisioning.
- Timing of decisions so actions happen before risk becomes an incident.
- Accountability across engineering, finance, and leadership stakeholders.

FinOps is an operating model and cultural practice connecting engineering,
finance, and business teams around technology value, cost visibility, timely
data-driven decisions, and financial accountability.

## 3. What capacity engineers communicate to FinOps
- Inform: show usage, trend, ownership, headroom, and risk windows.
- Optimize: identify right-sizing candidates, waste reduction, or remediation.
- Operate: convert review outcomes into recurring governance and action cadence.

Capacity engineers keep the discussion factual: what is trending, what is at
risk, when action is needed, and what tradeoffs exist.

## 4. Example meeting names
- Capacity and Cost Risk Review
- Forecast and Budget Alignment Review
- Platform Headroom and Spend Planning
- Service Demand and Financial Impact Review
- Monthly Engineering-Finance Planning Sync

## 5. Safe interview answer
"I was not the FinOps owner, but my capacity forecasting work produced the
telemetry, trend, headroom, and risk views that make FinOps conversations more
factual and actionable."

## 6. Apple lesson learned
At Apple, the lesson was that forecasting data is most useful when it is tied
to ownership and decision timing. Technical trend data alone is not enough.
When teams connect trend and headroom to planning windows and stakeholder
accountability, cost and capacity conversations become clearer and faster.

## 7. Risky wording to avoid
- "I owned FinOps end to end."
- "I ran all cloud cost governance decisions."
- "I owned the whole cloud platform budget model."
- "Forecasting replaced finance planning."

Use safer wording:
- "I supported FinOps-style reviews with capacity evidence and risk context."
- "I partnered with finance, platform, and application stakeholders."

## 8. One-minute spoken answer
Capacity forecasting can support FinOps-style planning because it gives teams a
shared view of usage trend, headroom, ownership, and risk timing. I would not
claim I owned FinOps. My role was to produce reliable forecast and risk views so
finance, platform, application, and leadership teams could make earlier and more
informed budget and remediation decisions. That made cost-risk tradeoffs more
factual and less reactive.

## 9. How to connect this back to the 30-2-5 forecasting story
- Keep capacity forecasting as the main storyline.
- Add the FinOps bridge only if asked about budget, cost, planning, or
  stakeholder communication.
- Link forecasting output to shared decision language: usage trend, headroom,
  ownership, risk timing, and action windows.
- Close by restating ownership boundaries: forecast support role, not FinOps
  ownership role.
